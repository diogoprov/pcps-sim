# Referee report — Methods in Ecology and Evolution

**Manuscript:** "Tree shape and metacommunity assembly govern the performance of phylogenetic community–environment methods"

**Article type:** Research Article

**Recommendation:** Major revision

---

## 1. Summary of the submission

The manuscript asks whether tests that relate the phylogenetic structure of metacommunities to an environmental gradient keep their published performance under conditions that the original validations did not cover. The tests are PERMANOVA and Mantel tests on phylogeny-weighted composition (matrix **P**), linear models on the first two PCPS axes, RDA on PCPS1–2 and the PCC, all with a site/taxa "max test". The authors build a 3 × 3 grid of tree shapes that crosses the γ statistic with a normalized Colless index, taking the levels from anuran clades in the Portik et al. (2023) tree. They cross this grid with eight trait scenarios in a static layer (Layer A, the Gaussian-response protocol of Peres-Neto et al. 2012) and in a process-based layer (Layer B, Thompson et al. 2020 dynamics with species sorting or mass effects, with or without phylogenetically structured competition). A third component plants a clade with shifted optima and compares how often PCPS, PCC, evoPCA and phylofactorization recover it. Rates from 1,000 replicates per condition are summarized with beta-binomial GLMs in brms. The main claims are these. (i) Imbalance does not affect type I error. (ii) Phylogenetic signal in niche breadth inflates type I error for GLM PCPS2 and the PCC. (iii) Strongly negative γ reduces the power of GLM PCPS1 and the PCC but not of PERMANOVA. (iv) Mass effects remove most power. (v) Branch-wise methods (evoPCA, phylofactorization) recover planted clades far better than PCPS or PCC, and their recovery does not depend on γ.

## Overall assessment

This is a carefully executed and unusually transparent simulation study. It is clearly within MEE's scope, and many empirical users of PCPS and the PCC would find it useful. Several aspects of the design are commendable. The validation against Duarte et al. (2016) and Peres-Neto & Kembel (2015) is careful. Trees are paired across scenarios. Niche breadth and abundance signal are imposed through ranks, so the marginal distributions do not change. The authors report Monte Carlo standard errors, declare explicit priors and diagnostics, write down the plan before the main runs, and share an anonymized code archive. The topic is also original: I know of no study that has crossed branching-time distribution with imbalance for these tests, or benchmarked clade recovery against a known answer.

However, I think several of the central inferences are not yet supported at the strength at which they are stated. Four problems drive my recommendation. (a) The levels of γ are confounded with N, and in Layer B the pool tree is confounded with the analysed tree. This is more serious than the text acknowledges: γ of the analysed tree also shifts by about 3.5 units under mass effects with competition. (b) In the code archive, the inflation of GLM PCPS2, a headline result, depends on an implementation choice for the taxa-shuffle null (no Procrustes alignment). The archive computes but the manuscript does not report the alternative. (c) The beta-binomial models act mainly as smoothers of a misspecified mean structure. Several numbers in the text are averages over cells but are presented as ranges over conditions. (d) Tree shape changes the data-generating distribution of optima as well as the analysis, which weakens the mechanistic interpretation. None of these problems is fatal. Most can be addressed with analyses the authors have already run or can run cheaply. The title and some recommendations should also be scaled back. "Tree shape … govern[s]" overstates a result in which one of the two shape axes had no detectable effect.

## 2. Major comments

**M1. Framing, novelty and title.** The study is novel in crossing γ with imbalance and in using a known-answer clade benchmark. Still, the title ("Tree shape and metacommunity assembly govern…") does not match the main result: imbalance had no detectable effect on type I error and at most a 0.09 effect on power. "Branching times" or "the distribution of node depths" would be more accurate. The Introduction states three gaps but never explains why γ, rather than another descriptor of node-depth distribution, is the relevant axis for methods based on **P**. The weights in **P** depend on branch lengths through the similarity matrix, not on γ per se. Please motivate γ mechanistically, or present it explicitly as a convenient summary of a richer property.

**M2. γ levels are confounded with N.** Table S1 shows that the "low γ" level is −5.58, −6.54 and −8.92 at N = 50, 100 and 200 (median: −3.79, −4.94, −6.20). γ scales with the number of tips, so this is perhaps unavoidable. The consequence is that every statement of the form "largest … with low γ and 200 species" (Abstract; Results, "0.21 (0.19–0.23) with low γ at N = 200"; SI, "the loss of GLM PCPS1 increased with N") mixes an effect of N with an effect of a more extreme γ. Please (i) explain how levels were derived per N from clades of 40–250 tips, (ii) consider a γ scale that is comparable across N (e.g., quantiles of the Yule null distribution of γ for each N, or a standardized node-depth statistic), and (iii) rephrase N-dependent conclusions accordingly.

**M3. Layer B: pool tree versus analysed tree.** The Results state that "under mass effects the Colless index of the analysed tree was about 75% lower than that of the pool". I recomputed the shapes from `data/derived/layerB/results.csv` at N = 100. Two further problems appear.
- γ also changes strongly. In the Yule/Yule pool cell, mean γ of the analysed tree is −0.03 under sorting, −1.51 under sorting with phylogenetic competition, −0.18 under mass effects and −3.48 under mass effects with phylogenetic competition. A "Yule pool tree" under mass effects plus competition therefore yields analysed trees near the median γ level. Because γ is the axis that mattered in Layer A, the "partial restoration" of power by competition cannot be attributed to the process, or to the change in imbalance alone.
- "75%" is not a meaningful summary of an index normalized to a Yule expectation of 0. In the Yule-Colless cells, the analysed tree goes from about −0.03 to −0.40, i.e., more balanced than Yule. In the mid/mid cell the drop is about 86%.

Pool sizes also differ widely among archetypes (about 100, 107, 177 and 468 species for N = 100). The analysed species are thus a different and non-random sample of the pool. Please report pool and analysed γ and Colless for every archetype × cell (a small table in the SI), and model or condition power on the analysed shape. One option is to add the realized γ/Colless as covariates in the Layer B model. Another is to resample analysed trees so that their shape matches across archetypes. Fig. 4 should also state that the x-axis refers to the pool tree.

**M4. GLM PCPS2 inflation and the taxa-shuffle null.** In `R/pcps_glm.R` the taxa-shuffle null for each PCPS axis takes the k-th axis of each permuted **P** without rotating it onto the observed axes. The code comment justifies this by noting that, with a single axis, `pcps.sig`'s Procrustes reduces to a sign/scale change. In Layer B the pipeline also computed `glm_pcps1_proc` and `glm_pcps2_proc`, with symmetric Procrustes of null axes 1–2 onto the observed axes. These variants are never mentioned in the manuscript. Under species sorting without competition, the rejection rate of GLM PCPS2 in S2σ is 0.068 (N = 100) and 0.096 (N = 200) without Procrustes, against 0.009 and 0.011 with Procrustes. The Procrustes variant is conservative throughout (S2: about 0.01 for PCPS2 and 0.026 for PCPS1), so it is not simply "the correct test". Still, the comparison shows that the inflation of PCPS2 comes from how axis identity is handled under the taxa shuffle, for example axis switching between PCPS1 and PCPS2 across permutations, rather than being an inherent property of PCPS. The diagnostic script `analysis/09_diag_s2sigma.R` also points to an arch effect. Its output shows that |r(PCPS2, E²)| ≈ 0.68–0.80 in low-γ trees, against |r(PCPS2, E)| ≈ 0.33–0.58. PCPS2 may therefore be largely the quadratic arch of PCPS1, in which case a linear test of PCPS2 on E is conceptually ill-posed. This would also explain why GLM PCPS2 has essentially no power (≤ 0.04 in every S3 cell of Fig. 3) yet "inflated" type I error. Please report the Procrustes variant (and RDA on PCPS1–2, which the archive suggests controls type I in Layer A) alongside the plain GLMs, discuss axis switching and the arch, and revise the corresponding recommendation (Discussion, recommendation iii).

**M5. Tree shape changes the data, not only the analysis.** Optima are Brownian (λ = 1 in S3b) on the tree and then rescaled to [−1, 101]. On low-γ trees, BM optima form a few tight clusters separated by deep splits, so the realized distribution of optima along E (gaps, clumping) differs from Yule trees. The power losses of GLM PCPS1 and the PCC may therefore reflect a gradient that is sampled less evenly, as well as the eigenstructure of the analysed matrix (the mechanism proposed in the Discussion). The authors already impose marginals by ranks for σ and h. Doing the same for u (fixed marginal, phylogenetic arrangement only) would separate the two explanations. The authors acknowledge that they "did not record the eigenvalue spectra of each replicate", yet the archive has `eig_share1`/`eig_share2` for Layer B and for the axes run. These data could at least test the mechanism partly.

**M6. Formal models.** I have four concerns.
- *Purpose.* With K = 1,000 per condition, the raw rates already have MCSE ≤ 0.016. The beta-binomial adds smoothing and interval estimates for contrasts, but the "overdispersion" of 2.1–8.5 is not extra-binomial variation among replicates: each condition is binomial by construction. It is lack of fit of the mean structure, i.e., omitted higher-order interactions. The model then shrinks extremes. This is why the text has to "report the raw rates where they exceed the modelled ones" (e.g., GLM PCPS2 in S2σ, low γ, N = 200: the raw rate is 0.356, against a modelled S2σ − S2 contrast of 0.21 on an S2 baseline of about 0.05–0.06). Please either fit the saturated cell-means model (equivalent to raw rates with binomial intervals) and use the structured model only for contrasts, or justify why a misspecified mean with a single φ yields calibrated CIs for contrasts.
- *Pairing.* Scenarios are paired by tree ("replicate r of every scenario used tree r"), but the models treat conditions as independent. Paired contrasts such as S2σ − S2 could be estimated from replicate-level discordant pairs, with much smaller uncertainty.
- *Priors.* normal(0, 1.5) on every coefficient of a heavily interacted logit model implies a fairly diffuse and non-obvious prior on cell rates. The normal(−2.9, 1.5) intercept depends on the contrast coding, which is not stated. A prior predictive check, or a plot of implied prior rates per cell, would help. gamma(2, 0.01) on φ (mean 200, SD about 141) is described as "weakly informative around … φ of about 130–910". Please state whether results are sensitive to it.
- *PPC.* In-sample PPC coverage of 96–98% (95% intervals) and 61–70% (50% intervals), with all grouped sums inside their 95% intervals, indicates over-dispersed predictions. These diagnostics cannot show local misfit in the cells of interest. Please show residuals (observed − predicted) for the cells where the conclusions are drawn.

**M7. Equivalence claims, Monte Carlo error and multiplicity.** H1 is rejected because "the difference … was within ±0.012, and no 95% CI excluded zero" across 75 combinations. The ±0.012 refers to posterior medians. The corresponding 95% CIs in `data/derived/models/contrasts.csv` span −0.030 to +0.020, so the design cannot exclude imbalance effects of about 0.02–0.03 on type I error. Absence of evidence is being read as evidence of absence. Please define an equivalence margin a priori (for example the Bradley interval, or ±0.01) and report the proportion of the posterior inside it. More generally, the paper reports hundreds of contrasts (814 rows in `contrasts.csv`) and highlights the largest ones. Please state how contrasts were chosen for the main text, and present the full set systematically (e.g., a forest-plot figure in the SI). Two "colless high − yule" type I contrasts (GLM PCPS1, S2h, N = 200) have CIs excluding zero, which sits uneasily with "no 95% CI excluded zero".

**M8. Are H1–H4 tested as stated?**
- H1 concerns the taxa shuffle, but the main analysis and Fig. 2 report the max test. The taxa-shuffle-only analysis is relegated to SI and reported only as counts of exceedances of MC intervals ("4 of 60").
- H2 predicts inflation of "all tests", "with a magnitude that depends on tree shape". Two tests were inflated, with shape dependence, and three became slightly conservative. "H2 was rejected as stated" is fair, but the more useful statement is that H2 was supported for GLM PCPS2 and the PCC and refuted for the others. Layer B also shows PCC inflation in S2h (raw up to 0.143 under mass effects, N = 200). This contradicts the Layer A conclusion that signal in maximum abundance "did not change the rejection rate of any test", and it is not discussed in the main text. Note that h is replaced by r_j in Layer B, so S2h is not the same scenario in both layers. This should be stated where H2 is evaluated.
- H3 rests on the claim that negative γ "would concentrate variation on the first PCPS axis". The authors find the opposite (SI). That is a valuable negative result, but it means the planted-clade result for H3 needs a different mechanism, and the definitions of "basal" and "recent" also change with γ (see M10).
- H4 is supported for power partly through a change in rank among tests whose CIs overlap. Please report the posterior probability of each rank ordering rather than ranks of medians.

**M9. Max test, conservativeness and comparability of power.** In S0 every test has a rejection rate of about 0.00–0.03 (Fig. 2; mean 0.011 across S0 conditions in Table S2), because both components must be rejected. That is the intended behaviour of the max test. However, it means that power is determined by the weaker of the two components, and that differences in power among tests may reflect which component limits them. Please report site- and taxa-shuffle power separately for at least the S3b conditions, so readers can see whether low γ hurts the taxa component (the expected case) or the site component.

**M10. Planted-clade design.** I have four concerns.
- Basal and recent clades are defined as "the older or … the younger half of the eligible nodes". In low-γ trees almost all nodes are old, so a "recent" clade on a low-γ tree has a stem much older in absolute (or relative) time than a recent clade on a Yule tree. Depth is therefore confounded with γ. Reporting the stem age and stem-branch length of planted clades by cell would help.
- There is no δ = 0 baseline. The chance hit rate of each scoring rule (best of about N − 1 nodes, with the complement counted as a hit) is not given. Without it, "no method exceeded 0.21" at δ = 0.5 cannot be interpreted.
- The three ordinations choose "the axis most correlated with E among the first four", which uses E in the selection step. Phylofactorization uses its own objective. This asymmetry should be acknowledged.
- The PCC is not designed to locate clades. Scoring it with eigenvector loadings gives it a role it was never proposed for, so its poor performance is unsurprising and should be framed as such. Node-based analysis (Borregaard et al. 2014) is cited as a natural comparator. It would be a stronger baseline than PCC for this aim.

**M11. Layer B realism: dispersal, incidence and number of sites.** Global dispersal with a = 0.5 is an extreme mass-effects regime. With 100 exchangeable sites, many species will occupy nearly every site. Analysing incidence only then discards almost all information, and the reported power losses of 0.55–0.93 may largely reflect this. Please report mean occupancy and among-site beta diversity per archetype, and analyse abundance in at least a subset of Layer B conditions. The Discussion acknowledges that the uniform kernel "probably represents the most extreme case". The Abstract and recommendation (iv) should carry the same qualification. Similarly, 100 sites and a single gradient length are fixed. A small sensitivity run (e.g., 30 and 300 sites, the range of the case-study data and of typical surveys) would greatly increase the practical value of the recommendations. The case-study metacommunities have 13–40 ponds, well below the simulated 100.

**M12. Reproducibility and reporting.** The archive is commendable. Some items expected under Williams et al. (2024) are missing:
- the handling and counts of failed or degenerate replicates (empty sites dropped, species pruned, PCC failures, trees rejected);
- whether incidence and abundance analyses use the same metacommunities. "432,000 metacommunities" appears to count each metacommunity twice, once per data type;
- the git commit or tag of the planning document and a table of departures from the plan. These are currently scattered through Appendix S1;
- an explicit statement of which tests were run but not reported (the Procrustes GLMs, RDA PCPS in Layer A).

The planning document was first committed on 16 October 2026 and the pipeline seed is 20261018. Please state which runs (pilots, calibrations) preceded the plan.

## 3. Minor comments

1. **Abstract** (353 words) exceeds the 350-word limit slightly. Paragraph 3 mixes modelled contrasts and raw maxima ("up to 0.36"). Please say which is which.
2. **Abstract/Results, clade ranges.** "evoPCA and phylofactorization recovered a planted clade in 0.44–0.88 … PCPS in 0.12–0.42 and PCC in only 0.08–0.12". These are means over the nine grid cells by N and depth (I recover 0.44–0.88, 0.12–0.42 and 0.08–0.12 from Table S2 this way). Across cells, PCPS ranges from 0.02 to 0.71 and PCC from 0.03 to 0.20 (shift 2, incidence). Please state that these are cell-averaged.
3. **Results, "With a shift of 0.5, no method exceeded 0.21".** Also a cell average. Cell-level PCPS reaches 0.30 (low/Yule, N = 200, basal).
4. **Results, "high imbalance reduced recovery by 0.01–0.10 in all methods".** In Fig. 5 (low γ row), PCPS drops from 0.58 to 0.45 (basal) and 0.35 to 0.24 (recent). State the γ level at which the Colless contrast is evaluated, given the γ × Colless interaction.
5. **Results, S2σ contrast at N = 50.** "0.05 (0.04–0.06)". `contrasts.csv` gives 0.048 (0.035–0.062), i.e., 0.03–0.06. Also note that in Yule trees GLM PCPS2 is not inflated at N = 50 (−0.003) but is inflated at N = 200 (+0.07). The text reports only N = 100.
6. **Results, "the power of PERMANOVA did not change (0.01, −0.02 to 0.05)".** Fig. 3 shows 0.81 → 0.83 (+0.02). Fine within rounding, but please check that the figure and the contrast use the same Colless level.
7. **Results, "Surprisingly, adding phylogenetic competition…"; Discussion, "Interestingly…", "Unfortunately…".** Please remove evaluative adverbs (MEE style).
8. **Results, "Under the null hypothesis, no lower CI bound exceeded 0.075 at N = 100".** This is a weak criterion. SI shows raw rates up to 0.129 (GLM PCPS2) and 0.143 (PCC) at N = 200. Report Layer B type I in the main text with the same criterion as Layer A.
9. **Methods, Tree shapes.** "Because γ depends only on the intervals between nodes and the Colless index only on the topology, this construction controls the two axes separately". γ depends on internode intervals ordered by node rank, and that ordering is constrained by the topology (the linear extension in Appendix S1). Please show the realized γ–Colless correlation within the grid, or soften "separately" to "nearly independently". The code itself says "(near) independent control".
10. **Methods, acceptance window ±0.5.** The Yule × Yule cell is a truncated Yule distribution (acknowledged in SI). Please report the realized within-cell SD of γ and Colless, since a narrow window makes each cell a point in shape space.
11. **Methods, "Bradley's liberal criterion".** Bradley's interval is a robustness criterion, not a Monte Carlo criterion. Please keep the two ideas separate (choice of K versus acceptability of a rate).
12. **Methods, PCC.** "using the variance of the adjusted R² across phylogenetic eigenvectors as the test statistic". Please state whether this follows Peres-Neto & Kembel (2015) exactly, and how many eigenvectors are used.
13. **Methods, Layer B.** The statement that uniform dispersal "keeps the site shuffle a valid null model" is correct, but it should appear as an explicit design constraint in the limitations, since it is what forces the absence of spatial structure.
14. **Methods, software citations.** vegan, PCPS, phylofactor, targets and crew are cited only in the SI. MEE expects software used in the main analyses to be cited in the main text.
15. **Figure 1** uses "PCPS–GLM" and "I_C", whereas the text uses "GLM PCPS" and "Colless index". **Q** is shown but never defined in the text. RDA appears in panel 4 but was used only in Layer B. "a" (dispersal) and "α" (competition) are hard to tell apart at print size.
16. **Figure captions** all begin with "Notice that…". MEE captions should describe the content. Interpretation belongs in the text.
17. **Figs 2, 3 and 5** show posterior medians only. Add raw rates (e.g., as small text) or a companion SI figure with CIs. As it stands, a reader cannot tell which cell differences are distinguishable from MC error.
18. **Fig. 2 caption** says "GLM PCPS2 doubles its rejection rate in the low-γ cells of S2σ". Relative to what: Yule S2σ (0.08 → 0.16) or S2 (0.06 → 0.16)? Please be explicit.
19. **Fig. 4** orders cells "Yule/Yule, low/Yule, Yule/high, low/high, mid/mid", which is neither by γ nor by Colless. Ordering by γ would make the γ effect visible. Label the x-axis "Pool tree cell", as in the caption.
20. **Discussion, "Previous evaluations could not detect this".** Rephrase as "did not evaluate this".
21. **Discussion, "to the best of our knowledge, and based on searches of the literature, this is the first…"** Priority claims are discouraged. Describe the search or drop the claim.
22. **Discussion, recommendation (ii)** "prefer PERMANOVA … when the tree has very negative γ and the pool has 100 or more species". At N = 50 PERMANOVA also lost 0.03–0.17 with low γ (SI). The recommendation should follow the full results, and it depends on M2.
23. **References.** "References (verified)" as a heading, the subtitle "Full draft", the HTML comment and the unresolved placeholder in Colless (1982) ("[CITAR: conferir página final]") must be removed before submission. Beverton & Holt (1957) mixes the original and the 1993 reprint DOI.
24. **SI Appendix S2.** "slightly more than the 3.6 cells expected by chance": 6 vs 3.6 out of 72 cells is not tested. Also, the binomial interval should account for MC error in both the published and the new estimates.
25. **SI Appendix S2, PCC validation.** "seems to reflect a single favourable tree" is speculative. Report it as a possibility.
26. **SI Appendix S3, anonymity.** The case study cites "a previous analysis with PCPS (Provete, 2018)" and "unpublished data". Please check that this does not compromise double-anonymous review.

## 4. Comments on figures and Supporting Information

- **Fig. 1** is informative but dense. Panel 5 (heat map for inference) duplicates Figs 2–3. The panel could be dropped in favour of a clearer mapping between scenario rows and estimands.
- **Figs 2–3** would benefit from a diverging colour scale centred on 0.05 for type I error (Fig. 2), so that the conservative S0/S1 rows and the inflated S2σ cells are visible at a glance.
- **Fig. 5** shows only N = 100, incidence and shift 2. A compact SI figure across shifts would support the δ-related claims (see M10 for a δ = 0 baseline).
- **Fig. S1** is useful. It shows that grid targets differ by N (M2): the low-γ targets for N = 200 lie outside most empirical clades of comparable size. Consider drawing empirical clades by size class.
- **Table S2** in the rendered SI is only a summary by model. Fine, given the CSV, but the CSV needs a column dictionary (e.g., `g_low__c_high`, `adonis`, `sorting__phylo`).
- **Table S3**: the "Dispersion" column should be labelled as quasibinomial dispersion. Bulk ESS ≥ 835 for B_power (text says ≥ 908 across models). Please correct "bulk effective sample size ≥ 908" in Appendix S2.
- **Appendix S1** should contain a table of departures from the plan (planned vs. implemented, reason).

## 5. Questions to the author

1. How were the γ and Colless levels derived for each N from the clades of 40–250 tips? Were clades binned by size?
2. Which taxa-shuffle null did Duarte et al. (2016) and the PCPS package use for GLMs on single PCPS axes (one axis or Procrustes on several)? Which does a typical user get by default?
3. Is PCPS2 in Layer A mostly the arch of PCPS1? What fraction of replicates have |r(PCPS2, E²)| > |r(PCPS2, E)|?
4. In Layer B under mass effects, what are mean site occupancy and mean among-site Jaccard dissimilarity? Does power recover with abundance data?
5. What would the competition result look like after conditioning on the γ of the analysed tree rather than that of the pool?
6. What is the chance-level hit rate of each clade-recovery scoring rule under δ = 0?
7. Did any replicate fail (non-convergent dynamics, PCC eigen-decomposition failure, too few species after pruning), and how were failures handled?
8. Why does PCC inflate under S2h in Layer B but not in Layer A? Is this attributable to r_j replacing h_j?
